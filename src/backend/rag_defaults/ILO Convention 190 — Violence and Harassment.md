# ILO Convention 190 — Violence and Harassment in the World of Work

Reference document. Violence and harassment at work is a **related labour-standards concern**: it frequently accompanies fundamental-rights violations (anti-union intimidation, discrimination, forced labour) and reinforces documentation. It is not the core of the interview and should be followed only when it arises in the user's own account.

## Instrument

- ILO Convention No. 190 (Violence and Harassment Convention, 2019), adopted June 2019, in force 25 June 2021.
- Accompanied by Recommendation No. 206 (2019), which gives non-binding guidance on implementation.
- The first international treaty to recognise the right of everyone to a world of work free from violence and harassment.

## Definition

"Violence and harassment" in the world of work means a range of unacceptable behaviours and practices, or threats thereof, whether a single occurrence or repeated, that aim at, result in, or are likely to result in physical, psychological, sexual or economic harm. It includes **gender-based violence and harassment** (directed at persons because of their sex or gender, or affecting persons of a particular sex or gender disproportionately, including sexual harassment).

## Who is protected

Workers and other persons in the world of work, regardless of contractual status: employees, persons in training (including interns and apprentices), volunteers, jobseekers and applicants, workers whose employment has been terminated, and individuals exercising the authority, duties or responsibilities of an employer. It applies to the public and private sectors, the formal and informal economy, and urban and rural areas.

## Where it applies

In the course of, linked with or arising out of work: at the workplace; in places where the worker is paid, takes a rest or meal, or uses sanitary/washing/changing facilities; during work-related trips, travel, training, events or social activities; through work-related communications (including those enabled by information and communication technologies); in employer-provided accommodation; and when commuting to and from work.

## Core obligations of ratifying states

- Adopt an inclusive, integrated and gender-responsive approach to prevent and eliminate violence and harassment.
- Respect, promote and realise the right to a world of work free from violence and harassment.
- Require employers to take steps to prevent violence and harassment: adopt a workplace policy; take account of violence and harassment (and associated psychosocial risks) in occupational safety and health management; identify hazards and assess risks; provide information and training; and provide accessible complaint and remedy mechanisms with protection from victimisation and retaliation.

## Relevance for classification

Harassment or violence at work maps to C190. It commonly overlaps with fundamental principles — for example intimidation of union members (freedom of association), harassment based on a protected characteristic (discrimination), or coercion within forced-labour situations. Document it where it emerges; treat it as reinforcing evidence rather than as a stand-alone investigative target.
