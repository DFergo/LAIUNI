# Right to Strike — Attribution of Responsibility

Interpretive aid for correctly attributing responsibility in strike-related situations. Its purpose is to prevent both under- and over-attribution: not every restriction of the right to strike is attributable to a company. Do not raise the topic of strikes proactively; use this guidance when a strike-related thread arises in the user's account.

## The right

The right to strike is part of freedom of association. It is recognised in the jurisprudence of the ILO Committee on Freedom of Association and the Committee of Experts on the Application of Conventions and Recommendations, in connection with Convention No. 87, and it is included within the freedom-of-association cluster of the EU CSDDD Annex.

## Attribution principle

Restrictions on the right to strike are frequently the action of the **State** — non-recognition of the right in law, statutory limits or bans, requisitioning, or policing of strike action. Where a restriction or repression of a strike is **purely state action**, it is generally **not attributable to the company**, even though the company may benefit from it.

Company responsibility arises where the **company actively participates in, instigates or benefits from the repression** of lawful strike action. Examples include:
- deploying private or public security personnel against strikers;
- soliciting or procuring police or military intervention against strikers;
- dismissing, sanctioning, transferring or blacklisting workers for participating in a strike;
- hiring replacement workers specifically to break a lawful strike;
- other retaliation against strikers or their representatives.

## Guidance for documentation

- Distinguish clearly between (a) State conduct that is not attributable to the company and (b) conduct by the company or instigated by the company.
- Document the company's **specific role** in the events. Where the company merely operates in a jurisdiction that restricts strikes, that is context, not a company-attributable violation.
- Absent company participation, a strike-repression situation may fall outside what is attributable to the company, while remaining relevant background to the case.
- Base the assessment on the company's actual role as described — do not assume company responsibility from the existence of a strike or its repression alone.
